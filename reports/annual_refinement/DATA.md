# Reading the North Atlantic refinement tables

Each model month has its own physical maps and wind dynamics. A member-year supplies one January, one February and so on. Inference uses the saved weights and a prescribed annual global warming level in kelvin relative to 1850–1900. The domain is 0–60°N, 100°W–0°. The tropical cyclone corridor used for the field scores is 10–30°N, 80–20°W.

## Field and wind scores

In the paired field tables, `baseline` is the published v1 LIM and `candidate` is the frozen research model. `difference` is candidate minus baseline. The same MPI member and year receive the same bootstrap weight for both models and all months. The primary 95% interval uses percentiles of 2,000 resamples. The independent tables also give a basic bootstrap interval reflected around the observed difference. A warming group containing one year supplies member uncertainty only. The column `valid_resamples` counts draws with a defined comparison. [New annual panel](data/paired_fresh.csv) · [Eleven-month sensitivity without September](data/paired_fresh_excluding_september.csv) · [Original test panel](data/paired_test.csv) · [All months of SSP2-4.5 in 2050](data/paired_ssp245_year.csv)

| Quantity | Inputs provided to the model | Interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `projection_rmse` | True ocean map encoded into its retained scores | Map error after decoding, in K for SST, metres for MLD, or K per 100 m for the subsurface gradient |
| `crps` | Global warming | Continuous ranked probability score of the predicted physical distribution, integrated over 20 equally spaced quantile probabilities. Lower is better. |
| `mean_rmse`, `coverage90` | Global warming | Error of the predicted ensemble mean and observed fraction within its central 90% interval |
| `monthly_wind_rmse` | Warming and the actual monthly MPI background | Area-weighted RMSE of the predicted monthly steering wind in m/s |
| `forcing_only_rmse` | Warming alone | Error of the expected monthly steering wind after averaging over background variability |
| `forecast_rmse` | Warming, actual background, two preceding daily wind states | Physical steering RMSE at a lead of 1, 5, 10 or 15 days. The table also scores persistence and the conditional monthly mean. |
| `variance_ratio` | Climate forcing and the fitted generative law | Generated steering variance divided by the MPI variance. One is exact. |
| `variance_log_error` | The same variance comparison | Absolute logarithm of the ratio, averaged across calendar months. Zero is exact. |

Steering is a vector with 20% weight on 250 hPa wind and 80% on 850 hPa wind. A `total` window keeps between-realization differences at the same grid point. A `within` window subtracts each realization’s monthly mean first. This gives zero variance for an entire centered month. `in_release_band` uses the original training-derived acceptance band. `closer_than_iid` compares absolute distances of the model and the matched independent-day variance ratios from one. [Window ratios, acceptance flags and Fourier controls](data/wind_windows_and_fourier.csv)

The native TC Fourier control has 15 harmonics with a 20-day fundamental period and a power exponent of 3. `candidate_tuned_Fourier` refits the two spectral parameters on the research model's training years. Every matched control has the same instantaneous wind covariance and monthly background as its parent LIM. In `paired_fourier` tables, the comparator label identifies the baseline. A negative difference in absolute log error favors the research LIM. [Fitted spectra](data/training_calibrated_fourier.csv) · [New-year paired spectral comparisons](data/paired_fourier_fresh.csv)

A calendar month passes the published 5-, 10- or 15-day test if its total steering variance ratio lies inside the original month-specific training band and closer to one than independent daily weather with the same background and instantaneous moments. The combined row requires all three windows to pass in that month. The counts retain the original bands for every method. A pass measures agreement with the sampled MPI panel and its tolerance. [Counts by warming and method](data/multiday_pass_counts.csv) · [Individual month ratios and bounds](data/wind_windows_and_fourier.csv)

## Ocean structure and background-to-wind covariance

The front-weighted EOF experiment uses the strongest fifth of adjacent-cell climatological gradients for each physical ocean field. All alternatives use 57 training years and retain the original 18 coarse modes in each block. Each validation member-month first contributes one area-weighted physical RMSE. The table reports the mean of those RMSEs across cases. The gradient column uses the field's own physical unit per 100 km horizontally. For paired uncertainty, the seven members and six years are resampled together across months. The public February grid includes the mean mixed-layer depth and gradient percentile used in the illustration. [Scores by field and warming](data/ocean_front_validation.csv) · [Paired intervals](data/ocean_front_validation_intervals.csv) · [Individual cases](data/ocean_front_validation_cases.parquet) · [February training grid](data/february_ocean_fronts.npz)

The regional file includes six boxes, threshold exceedance probabilities, upper-decile reconstruction errors and Brier scores. A grid cell's upper-decile threshold comes from original training years. The spatial dependence file compares increments over one, three and six grid steps and dependence among SST, MLD and the subsurface gradient. Regional MLD errors are in metres. The subsurface gradient is the cooling per 100 m measured over the first 100 m below the mixed layer. [Regional ocean scores](data/regional_ocean.csv) · [Spatial dependence](data/ocean_dependence.csv)

In the wind budget, `predicted_background` is the trace of the wind covariance inherited through the background regression. `predicted_monthly_weather` is the contribution of temporally averaged daily weather. At matched MPI member-years the table also applies the fitted regression to the actual backgrounds and measures the remaining variance and its covariance with the fitted wind. The per-field table gives each block's own-variance term and its signed covariance with every other block. Summing the four rows' `net_contribution` gives the total background contribution. [Wind variance budget](data/background_to_wind.csv) · [Per-field signed contributions](data/background_to_wind_by_field.csv)

The fixed-transfer covariance table holds the published EOF basis and wind regression constant. It compares six covariance laws fit with 15, 30 and 57 years against the same seven validation members. The four source blocks are SST, joint log-depth and gradient, pressure and the temperature–humidity profiles. The sum of all 16 signed terms is the basin steering variance induced by the observed backgrounds. `cross_error` is the absolute error in the combined ocean–atmosphere cross term divided by observed background-induced wind variance. `matrix_error` is the Frobenius error in the four-by-four table divided by the trace of its observed matrix. [Controlled comparison of covariance laws](data/fixed_transfer_covariance_validation.csv)

The daily forecast files compare physical steering RMSE after giving every driver the same two preceding daily maps and actual monthly background. The column `baseline` is the named comparator and `candidate` is the frozen LIM. The new year's monthly climate is presumed known. The dates at lead 1, 5, 10 and 15 stay inside the same calendar month. The bootstrap first averages the different starts within a member-month, then keeps all twelve months paired. [Conditional forecasts on new years](data/two_day_forecasts_fresh.csv) · [Conditional forecasts throughout 2050](data/two_day_forecasts_ssp245_year.csv)

The calendar-smoothing trial keeps the original EOFs and wind regression fixed and uses the 42 additional training years. Each of 64 replicates withholds seven members and five years, with three warming groups, three fitting-member counts and three fitting-year counts. The raw table has 248,832 rows, one per split, method, target and month, averaged across the five held years. The summary averages across months and replicates. The signed error is log predicted variance divided by measured variance. Absolute log error uses the absolute value within each held year. Overlapping draws share training cases, so the fraction beating the unpooled fit describes these draws. [Calendar-smoothing summary](data/seasonal_covariance_training_cv.csv) · [Raw calendar-smoothing folds](data/seasonal_covariance_training_folds.parquet)

The equal-rank front-to-circulation comparison refits the same conditional ridge regression for the three front weights. Pressure, profile and wind coordinates are shared. Each winter or summer month retains its own frozen daily weather law. For each validation year, the 15-day target separates ensemble-mean sampling noise using the same finite-member variance correction for all three methods. The background-variance target applies each method’s fitted response to the actual MPI backgrounds. [Physical wind and variance summary](data/front_circulation_validation.csv) · [Yearly variance budgets](data/front_circulation_years.csv) · [Individual monthly wind cases](data/front_circulation_cases.parquet)

The deep-layer table gives quantile loss in metres and the threshold Brier score in squared probability. For quantiles, `expected_rate` is the nominal upper-tail target and `observed_rate` is the area-weighted fraction of deeper MPI values. For thresholds of 1,000, 2,000 and 3,000 m, the expected rate is the analytic model probability. The statistical ceiling uses cellwise calendar-month maxima found over 57 training years. The subpolar box spans 45–60°N and 65–20°W. [Tail scores](data/deep_mixed_layer_validation.csv) · [Member-month details](data/deep_mixed_layer_validation_cases.parquet) · [Tail-score intervals by warming](data/deep_mixed_layer_validation_intervals.csv)

The wind-visible gain experiment uses the 57-year research coordinates and monthly wind regression. The training target is their empirical transmitted steering variance across 35 MPI members within a year. The gain multiplies only the model covariance in directions seen by the wind. The design evaluates four corrections alongside the original model on the same seven validation members. The 15-day target is at fixed climate with the finite-member correction. The archive provides all 684 physical training targets, 60 fitted month-by-method parameter rows, and the six validation years for every month and method. [Training targets](data/wind_visible_gain_training_targets.csv) · [Fitted gains](data/wind_visible_gain_fits.csv) · [Yearly variance budget](data/wind_visible_gain_yearly.csv) · [Individual ocean scores](data/wind_visible_gain_ocean_cases.parquet)

## Directional wind memory and tropical cyclones

The lagged matrix uses a future component on the row and an earlier component on the column. `u250`, `v250`, `u850` and `v850` denote eastward and northward winds at the stated pressures in hPa. The matrix archive contains physical covariances in m²/s². The interval tables divide by observed component standard deviations and summarize errors in correlation units. The `directional` score measures the six different pairs in the antisymmetric part of the matrix. [Lagged matrices](data/leadlag_matrices_fresh.csv) · [Paired matrix errors](data/leadlag_intervals_fresh.csv) · [Derivation](../../experiments/annual_refinement/METHODS.md)

Storm rates use every attempted disturbance as the denominator. Accepted storms have six-hour fixes. The displacement statistic requires an available 48-hour segment and the intensity statistic uses lifetime maximum wind. A Wasserstein-1 distance is the average shift along a one-dimensional distribution required to match the MPI-driven reference. The units are kilometres for displacement and m/s for wind. In the seasonal and annual storm tables, a driver name containing `_gain_vs_` gives the named comparator's error minus the research model's error. Positive values then favor the research LIM. A missing distance means one side lacked a qualifying storm. The annual replay gives every calendar month equal seeding effort. Converting its formation rates to expected basin counts would require a calibrated climatology of disturbances. Each 15-day trajectory stays within a calendar month. [Annual 2050 replay](data/annual_mpi2050_storms.csv) · [All attempted disturbances](data/annual_mpi2050_attempts.parquet) · [Every accepted six-hour track](data/annual_mpi2050_tracks_6h.parquet) · [Larger September 2050 replay](data/paired_mpi2050.csv) · [July, September and November validation replay](data/paired_seasonal_storms.csv)

## Training trials and reproducibility

The paired training-size steps keep the reference model, held years, held members and training-member count the same in each split. They give the mean of each split’s relative change, with an additional direct 30-to-57-year comparison. The fraction of improved splits describes sensitivity across overlapping training sets. [Exact changes between training sizes](data/matched_training_steps.csv) · [Individual fit scores with held and fitting member/year lists](data/training_partition_scores.parquet)

The residual-ocean validation table isolates uncertainty discarded by an EOF truncation. Each training year has its ensemble mean removed. The table compares the same checkpoint with zero, 16, 64 or all residual modes per ocean block and a 64-mode temperature-dependent scale. The score uses the original seven validation members. `extra_fraction` is the fraction of the discarded within-year variance retained by the added modes, in the training map metric. The 90% coverage and CRPS columns are measured in physical space. [Unresolved ocean variability](data/unresolved_ocean_variance_validation.csv)

The reconstruction screens use the same seven validation members and six years across ranks and methods. The screen’s upper-tail threshold and climatology can depend on the fitting cohort. Frozen-checkpoint regional scores use a common original-training threshold. The matched learning-curve file compares 15, 30 and 57 years with the same evaluation members, years and forcing range within each split. Repeating a split across training sizes gives a paired sensitivity measurement. The published intervals for independent climates resample actual members and years.
